# Week 1 Project Scope

| Field | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Project Name** | FloodWise MY: A Source-Cited Assistant for Malaysian Flood Preparedness Information |
| **Domain** | Disaster management and public safety |
| **Problem Statement** | During floods, Malaysian households must check information from different places, such as river level readings, weather warnings, relief centre lists and safety advice. Each comes from a different agency and is updated at a different speed. Reading all of this under time pressure is slow, and people can miss important details. Families in flood-prone states, elderly residents and parents with young children are affected most. An AI-based solution is suitable because people ask questions in free-form, mixed Malay and English, and the answer often has to be put together from several documents. FloodWise MY retrieves passages from official documents and writes an answer limited to those passages, with a citation for each claim. It gives information only and does not replace instructions from NADMA or local authorities. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** NADMA reported that the number of evacuees rose to 37,328 across nine states as of 6am on 29 November 2025, with 327 temporary relief centres operating. MetMalaysia had also issued a red-level continuous rain warning for several areas.<br>**Source / citation:** New Straits Times (2025, 29 November). *Flood situation worsens nationwide, evacuees rise to more than 37,000*. https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2025/11/1325516/flood-situation-worsens-nationwide-evacuees-rise-more-37000<br><br>**Evidence 2:** On 1 December 2025, 14,040 evacuees were still in relief centres. JKM reported that 2,101 were senior citizens, 4,745 were children (including 375 infants) and 499 were persons with disabilities. Many of the people affected need clear and simple information.<br>**Source / citation:** The Vibes (2025, 1 December). *Over 14,000 flood evacuees remain in relief centres across eight states*. https://www.thevibes.com/articles/news/116328/over-14000-flood-evacuees-remain-in-relief-centres-across-eight-states |
| **Initial Related Work (at least 3)** | **Study / solution 1:** DAISY, a disaster information chatbot that compares a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 Turbo model with a Zephyr-7B RAG system, using real-time data from GDACS.<br>**Source / citation:** He, A., Pelaia, D., He, N., Jones, S., & Zhu, F. (2026). *DAISY: A Comparative Evaluation of Fine-Tuned GPT-3.5 and Zephyr-RAG for Natural Disaster Information Dissemination*. The National High School Journal of Science. https://nhsjs.com/2026/daisy-a-comparative-evaluation-of-fine-tuned-gpt-3-5-and-zephyr-rag-for-natural-disaster-information-dissemination/<br>**Key limitation:** It covers disasters worldwide using sources such as FEMA, USGS and WHO, so it is not built for Malaysian agencies or flood warnings. The authors also note that it was tested with only five questions per disaster type, and not with real users.<br><br>**Study / solution 2:** Clara, a disaster response chatbot from the American Red Cross that gives emergency response guidelines.<br>**Source / citation:** American Red Cross. *Meet Clara, the disaster response chatbot*. https://www.redcross.org/get-help/disaster-relief-and-recovery-services/meet-clara.html<br>**Key limitation:** The DAISY authors note that chatbots like this may not provide real-time data or full coverage. It is also not built around Malaysian agencies or languages.<br><br>**Study / solution 3:** A review of how large language models are used in disaster management.<br>**Source / citation:** Xu, F., Ma, J., Li, N., & Cheng, J. C. P. (2025). Large language model applications in disaster management: an interdisciplinary review. *International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction, 127*, 105642. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijdrr.2025.105642<br>**Key limitation:** It is a review, so it does not provide a working system for Malaysian flood information. It is also cited by the DAISY authors when they stress that accuracy and reliability matter in high-stakes settings. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | **Gap / Limitation:** The related work shows that RAG chatbots can give cited disaster information, but I did not find one built on Malaysian flood sources. The existing evaluations are also small (DAISY used five questions per disaster type) and do not test whether each citation really supports the answer. Flood data also goes out of date quickly, so a wrong or old answer can be harmful. This project addresses this with citations for each claim, abstention when evidence is weak, a visible "data last checked" date, and evaluation on a manually verified question set. |
| **Initial Project Objectives (2 to 3)** | **O1:** To design a document-grounded assistant for Malaysian flood preparedness information based on official documents.<br>**O2:** To develop the assistant using retrieval-augmented generation, citation checking, and guardrails that make it abstain when evidence is insufficient.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the assistant using 30 to 50 benchmark questions, measuring retrieval relevance, information accuracy, citation correctness, unsupported-claim rate, and response latency. |
| **Target Users** | Households in flood-prone areas of Malaysia, community leaders, and relief centre volunteers. Elderly users and caregivers of young children are a priority group. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | **Source 1:** ReliefWeb, Malaysia Floods Nov 2025. URL: https://reliefweb.int/disaster/FL-2025-000211-MYS<br>**Source 2:** ReliefWeb, Malaysia Floods Nov 2024. URL: https://reliefweb.int/disaster/fl-2024-000218-mys<br>**Source 3:** Public InfoBanjir (river levels). URL: https://publicinfobanjir.water.gov.my (to be confirmed in the Week 2 source audit) |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Both are AI advisors built for one specific user group. Both answer questions from a fixed set of documents and give users a simple chat interface. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | FloodWise MY works in a safety-critical setting, so a wrong answer can put people in danger, and it needs stricter citation and abstention rules. It also depends on fast-changing data, so every answer must show the data date, and it must work in English and Bahasa Malaysia. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | **Risk 1:** Hallucination or missed information. A wrong evacuation or safety tip could put people in danger. Mitigation: answer only from retrieved sources, cite them, and abstain when no source supports an answer.<br>**Risk 2:** Outdated data. Old flood levels or warnings could be mistaken for current ones. Mitigation: show a timestamp on every answer and link users to the live official portals.<br>**Risk 3:** Over-reliance. Users may trust the chatbot instead of official instructions. Mitigation: show a clear notice to follow NADMA and local authority orders. |
